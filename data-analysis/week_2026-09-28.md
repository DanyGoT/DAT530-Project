# Week study — Mon 28 Sep – Fri 2 Oct 2026

A detailed look at one week of the pipe yard, to set and check the Petri net model.
Sources: `receive.csv`, `Orders.csv`, `Workstation.csv`, `workstation_items.csv`, `serial_history.csv`,
`DTs.csv`, `Location.csv`, all exported 2026-10-23. For whole-period averages, see `reference_stats.md` (based on the old exports).

"Week" means Mon 00:00 to Sat 00:00 unless stated otherwise. No work was recorded on the weekend.

## TL;DR

- The week is **busy at both ends of the yard and quiet in the hall**. Deliveries ranked 2nd of 41 weeks in 2026, and receives were about the median. Hall scans ranked 39th of 41, and inspected joints 35th of 41.
- **Monday's receive spike is mostly a paperwork effect.** 1176 of the week's 1460 joints are tubing that was backdated to 28 Sep but entered on 1, 13 and 15 Oct. That tubing never passes through the inspection hall (scope "Inspection", no workstation scans).
- **Rig Return orders wait about 2 weeks before inspection** (median 14 days from receive to inspection completed). Inspection itself is fast: it finishes the same day or the next.
- **Hall scans are batch registrations**, not one joint at a time. 98.5% of scans in an order are less than 60 s apart, and most in the same second. They tell you when a batch entered the hall, not how long each joint took.
- **Inspection is signed off in batches.** Reports are transferred in runs about 2–3 min apart, typically 13:00–18:00, covering everything scanned that morning or the day before.
- **The machining share is high this week.** 65% of Rig Return joints inspected in the week went to machining (whole-period average: 35%). Hardbanding 0.9%, scrap 2.8%.

## 2. Inspection hall

**Scans** (`workstation_items`): 376 joints in 31 orders. The 2026 median is about 760 scans per week.

| Day | Scans | What |
|---|---:|---|
| Mon | 234 | morning: 11 small RR orders (DC/HW); midday: RtoR and Re Inspection batches; plus part of a 309-joint DP order |
| Tue | 52 | rest of the large DP order (late evening) |
| Wed | 42 | morning: 8 small DC orders; afternoon: 3 DP orders |
| Thu | 33 | one 31-joint HW order |
| Fri | 15 | two small orders |

- Joints per order: median 2, p75 10, max 309. The hall works through **many small orders**.
- Scans run 07:00–23:00 and cluster around 08:00, 10:00, 12:00–13:00 and 15:00–16:00. Some evening scans (20:00–23:00) belong to the one large DP order.
- Scans are **bulk entries**: within an order, the gap between scans has a median of 0 s, and 98.5% are under 60 s. So the hall's service time **cannot** be measured per joint from this data.
- `Line` is the joint's place in the physical sequence through the hall, counted within its order: order 1 joints 1, 2, 3, then order 2 joint 1, and so on. Sorting by order, then `Line`, gives the layout. Line 1 is furthest in, nearest the output: joints go in as a batch, so the first one in ends up deepest (inferred, not recorded).

**Sign-off** (`Workstation.Transfer`, "Report Transfered <timestamp>"):

- Time from last scan to report transfer: median 0.9 days, p25 0.2, p90 1.4, max 7.8.
- Transfers come in runs about 2–3 min apart. Examples: Mon 14:57–14:59; Tue 17:51 → 18:14 for 9 orders in a row; Wed 13:21 → 13:47 for 6. One person signs off the backlog in one sitting.

## Data quality notes

- `Returned_Date` can be backdated by up to 17 days compared with when the record was created (the tubing on 28 Sep).
- `workstation_items` timestamps are bulk scans and say nothing about how long each joint takes.
- `serial_history` mixes "state before the change" and "the change itself". `DT No` on shipping rows is stale, and `Current Location` is blank on about 37% of rows.
- Machining and hardbanding completion dates are almost never filled in after mid-2025. Time spent in Norse, Wepco or Hardbanding could only be estimated from `serial_history` relocation timestamps, which hasn't been done yet.
- In `DTs.csv`, 30% of joints this week have no equipment type and most tickets have no order link.
